StrappedThirty days in the system

You get three cards: something you have going for you, something in your way, and the life you are actually living. Then you get thirty days, three things you can do each day, and a system that wants documents you cannot get without other documents.

This is a teaching tool, not a service. It was built for funders, policymakers, students, volunteers and new staff — people who need to understand why the system produces the outcomes it does. If you are looking for actual help, skip this page and find resources or call 2-1-1.
Before you play, one thing. There is no timer on this page and nothing is stored. The scarcity you will feel is time and money, not urgency — take as long as you want on any day. Some situations in this simulation cannot reach a good outcome in thirty days. That is not a bug, and the debrief at the end will tell you which one you drew.

How it works

Each day you have three actions. Almost everything costs one or two of them, plus bus fare, plus energy you may not have. Offices are open when you are working. Phone calls need battery. Paperwork needs other paperwork.

You are not trying to get housed in thirty days — almost nobody does, and a simulation that let you would be lying to you. You are trying to make the specific kinds of progress that actually matter: get identity documents, get onto the coordinated entry list, hold onto income, stay connected, stay alive through a Phoenix summer.

Providers here are composites, not real organisations. The system is the real part.
