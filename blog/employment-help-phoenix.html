Free Job Help in Phoenix When You’re Experiencing Homelessness

By Common Ladder · August 16, 2026 · 7 min read

Work is usually not the first thing that breaks, and it is rarely the first thing that gets fixed. Most job-search advice assumes you have an address, a phone, a shower, clean clothes, and an ID sitting in a drawer. If you are unhoused in Phoenix, the job is often not the hard part — the five things stacked in front of the job are. This guide is organized around that stack. If you only want one number, dial 2-1-1 (free, confidential, 24/7) and say you are looking for work.

Start with the free job centers — and go to the right one

Arizona’s public workforce system, ARIZONA@WORK, is free and open to anyone. Maricopa County is split into two separate service areas, and walking into the wrong one costs you a bus trip.

If you live inside the city of Phoenix

  • South Job Center — 4635 S. Central Ave., Phoenix, AZ 85040 — (602) 495-6905
  • West Job Center — 3406 N. 51st Ave., Phoenix, AZ 85031 — (602) 495-6906

If you live elsewhere in Maricopa County

  • West Valley Career Center — 4425 W. Olive Ave., Suite 190, Glendale, AZ 85302 — (602) 372-4200
  • East Valley Career Center — 1001 W. Southern Ave., Suite 101, Mesa, AZ 85210 — (602) 372-9700

Smaller partner sites also operate in Avondale (623-333-2703), Surprise (623-222-1630), Mesa (480-769-7417), Tempe (602-419-6466), and a youth hub in Tempe (480-350-5824).

Both systems offer the same core services: one-on-one career advising, resume help, interview practice, job referrals, free workshops, and in some cases funding toward a certification in a high-demand occupation. The City of Phoenix runs recurring free workshops — resume writing, interview skills, career exploration — that are worth asking about when you call.

St. Joseph the Worker: the one built for this

If you are experiencing homelessness specifically, St. Joseph the Worker is the Phoenix organization designed around your situation. It has been doing this work since 1988.

  • Phoenix: 382 E. Palm Lane, Suite 150, Phoenix, AZ 85004
  • Mesa: 1833 W. Main St., Suite 128, Mesa, AZ 85201
  • Phone: 602-755-5627 (602-755-JOBS)
  • Hours: generally Monday–Thursday, 9 a.m.–noon and 1–4 p.m., closed Friday through Sunday. Call before you go.

They provide job coaching, a written job-search plan, and direct referrals to employers who hire people with rough histories. They also do the part most agencies don’t: interview clothes, work uniforms, steel-toed boots, hand tools, and transportation help. If your barrier is that you cannot show up looking employable, this is the door.

One thing to know before you go: to use their job-search service you need two forms of ID — a state photo ID plus a Social Security card or birth certificate — and a working phone. If you don’t have those yet, read the next section first.

Employment help on Key Campus

If you are already downtown, you don’t have to leave the block. St. Vincent de Paul Workforce Development operates employment services inside the Lodestar Day Resource Center on Key Campus (204 S. 12th Ave., Phoenix), alongside a job-focused computer lab run with PNC Bank. The same building houses the Homeless ID Project, which replaces birth certificates and works with the Arizona MVD to replace state IDs and driver licenses by on-site appointment. That matters here for a practical reason: it means the ID that St. Joseph the Worker requires and the job coaching you need are a short walk apart.

Clearing the stack: ID, phone, address, clothes, a ride

  • ID. This is the most common hard stop. Our Phoenix ID recovery guide covers who helps, and the free ID Recovery Wizard tells you which document to get first so you don’t get stuck in the loop where each one requires the others.
  • Phone. Nearly every agency here needs a number that reaches you. Ask a case manager about Lifeline, the federal program that discounts phone service for people receiving SNAP, AHCCCS, SSI, and other benefits.
  • An address and mail. Key Campus, run by Keys to Change (formerly the Human Services Campus), at 204 S. 12th Ave., Phoenix — (602) 229-5155 — is the downtown block where mail service, ID help, health care, legal aid, and employment services sit together. Its mailroom is the only dedicated post office for people experiencing homelessness in the country, and the address it gives you can be used on job applications. The Brian Garcia Welcome Center there is open 24 hours a day.
  • A shower and clean clothes. See our showers and hygiene guide. St. Joseph the Worker and Goodwill both supply interview clothing.
  • Getting there. Our transportation guide covers free and half-price bus passes, which agencies hand them out, and how to book a free medical ride.

Goodwill career centers

Goodwill of Central and Northern Arizona runs free career centers open to everyone — veterans and non-veterans alike. The center at 4005 N. 16th St., Phoenix, AZ 85016 (16th Street and Indian School) is open Monday–Thursday, 9 a.m.–4 p.m., closed for lunch from noon to 12:30, and closed Friday through Sunday. Reach Goodwill’s employment services line at 602-535-4444. Services include resume help, mock interviews, computer and soft-skills training, one-on-one career coaching, and on-site hiring events. A Disabled American Veterans service officer takes walk-ins at this location Tuesdays and Thursdays, 10 a.m.–2 p.m.

If you have a record

A felony narrows the field. It does not close it. The Arizona Department of Economic Security and the Department of Corrections, Rehabilitation and Reentry run a joint reentry employment program delivered through ARIZONA@WORK, including Second Chance Center job fairs stocked with background-friendly employers. Ask for reentry services by name at any job center — it is a specific program, not a general attitude, and asking for it directly gets you to the right advisor faster. In practice, Phoenix construction, warehousing, and logistics employers hire heavily on reliability rather than record.

What you can do today, for free, from any phone or library computer

  • Create a free account at azjobconnection.gov, Arizona’s public job board, and build a resume there. Any public library computer works.
  • To file for unemployment insurance, call 1-877-600-2722.
  • Save the numbers on this page to your My Ladder plan so they are in one place when you need them.
  • Browse our employment help and job training pages for more programs.

Work the stack in order. ID, then a phone, then clothes and a ride, then the job center. Skipping a step is usually what sends people home from an intake desk empty-handed.

Verify before you go / call ahead. Phone numbers, office hours, eligibility rules, and document requirements change, and several of these offices keep short or seasonal hours. Call before traveling to any address on this page. If a number is out of service, dial 211 for a current referral.

Have a correction or an update? Contact us — we verify and update this information when changes are reported.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I get free help finding a job in Phoenix if I’m homeless?

Start with St. Joseph the Worker, the Phoenix nonprofit built specifically for job seekers experiencing homelessness: 382 E. Palm Lane, Suite 150, Phoenix, or 1833 W. Main St., Suite 128, Mesa; call 602-755-5627. They provide job coaching, employer referrals, interview clothes, work uniforms, tools, and transportation help. Arizona’s public workforce system, ARIZONA@WORK, is also free and open to anyone, and Goodwill of Central and Northern Arizona runs free career centers. If you are not sure where to start, dial 2-1-1 from any phone.

Do I go to the City of Phoenix job center or the Maricopa County one?

It depends on where you live. If you live inside the city of Phoenix, use the City of Phoenix job centers: the South Job Center at 4635 S. Central Ave., (602) 495-6905, or the West Job Center at 3406 N. 51st Ave., (602) 495-6906. If you live elsewhere in Maricopa County, use the county career centers: West Valley at 4425 W. Olive Ave., Suite 190, Glendale, (602) 372-4200, or East Valley at 1001 W. Southern Ave., Suite 101, Mesa, (602) 372-9700. Call first so you don’t spend a bus trip on the wrong office.

Can I get help finding a job without an ID?

Some doors open without one and some do not. St. Joseph the Worker’s job-search service requires two forms of ID — a state photo ID plus a Social Security card or birth certificate — and a working phone. ARIZONA@WORK career advisors and 2-1-1 will still talk to you. If ID is your blocker, deal with that first: several Phoenix organizations help replace lost documents, and the free ID Recovery Wizard on this site tells you which document to get first.

What if I have a felony on my record?

Arizona DES and the Department of Corrections, Rehabilitation and Reentry run a joint reentry employment program through ARIZONA@WORK, including Second Chance Center job fairs stocked with background-friendly employers. Ask for reentry services by name at any job center. Phoenix construction, warehousing, and logistics employers in particular hire heavily on reliability rather than record.
