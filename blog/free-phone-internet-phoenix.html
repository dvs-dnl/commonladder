Free Phone and Internet in Phoenix: Lifeline, Free Wi-Fi, and Low-Cost Computers

By Common Ladder · September 11, 2026 · 7 min read

Have a phone, but the online steps are the hard part?

The Digital Literacy Quick-Start walks through the exact things benefit and housing systems now assume you can do — set up an email, attach a document, join a video hearing, upload to a portal — one short module at a time. No test, no score.

Open the Quick-Start →

A phone is no longer optional. Shelter callbacks, benefit interviews, housing-list check-ins, court dates, and job offers all assume someone can reach you — and most of them now assume an email address too. Losing a phone is one of the fastest ways a housing plan falls apart.

Here is how to get connected in Phoenix and Maricopa County for free or close to it, what the rules actually are, and one widely repeated piece of outdated advice to ignore.

Start here: Lifeline, the federal phone and internet discount

Lifeline is the federal program that discounts phone or internet service by $9.25 a month, or $34.25 a month for households on federally recognized Tribal lands. Several wireless companies build a plan around that discount, which is why you will see it advertised as a "free government phone" — the phone and the monthly plan are covered by the subsidy.

You qualify if either is true:

  • Your total household income is at or below 135% of the Federal Poverty Guidelines, or
  • You, your child, or anyone in your household receives SNAP, Medicaid (AHCCCS), Supplemental Security Income (SSI), Federal Public Housing Assistance, or the Veterans Pension and Survivors Benefit.

The rules that trip people up: one Lifeline benefit per household, not per person. It applies to either phone or internet, not both. And you have to recertify once a year — missing that notice is the most common reason service stops, so keep the address on the account current.

How to apply

  • Online: the National Verifier at lifelinesupport.org, which also lists the companies serving your ZIP code so you can choose one rather than take whichever brand is handing out phones at a table.
  • By phone or paper: call 1-800-234-9473 to ask for a paper application.
  • No permanent address? Lifeline accepts a temporary or shelter address. Use a mailing address where you can actually receive the recertification notice — our guide to getting a mailing address in Phoenix covers the options.

If you are signing up at a folding table in a parking lot, the offer may still be legitimate — many Lifeline providers do street enrollment — but write down the company name, keep your account number and PIN somewhere separate from the phone, and confirm the company appears on the official list at lifelinesupport.org before handing over documents.

Ignore anything that offers you "ACP"

The Affordable Connectivity Program — the $30-a-month internet discount that 23 million households used — ended on June 1, 2024 when its funding ran out. As of September 2026, Congress has not passed a federal replacement. The FCC's own post-ACP fact sheet confirms it.

A lot of printed flyers, agency handouts, and resource sites still list ACP as if you can sign up. You cannot. If someone tells you to apply for ACP, they are working from old information — ask about Lifeline instead.

Free Wi-Fi and free computers

Maricopa County Library District is the most useful option in the county, because you do not need a library card. Any branch will issue a free guest pass to use a public computer. Sessions run 30 to 60 minutes when other people are waiting, the computers have Office and multiple browsers, and your browsing history and downloads are deleted when you log out. The public Wi-Fi network reaches the parking lot while the branch is open, which matters if you need to get online outside opening hours. Printing is about $0.25 a page in black and white. Main line: (602) 652-3000.

Phoenix Public Library runs Burton Barr Central Library at 1221 N. Central Ave., Phoenix, AZ 85004, (602) 262-4636, plus 16 branches, all with free computers and Wi-Fi. Library cards are free.

If you need someone to sit with you and walk through a task, ask a Maricopa County branch about a Book-A-Librarian appointment — it is free one-on-one help, and librarians are used to exactly these questions.

Borrowing a device, or buying one cheap

  • Chromebook To-Go and Hotspot To-Go (Maricopa County Library District) — three-week loans of a laptop or a mobile hotspot. Requires a library card in good standing, 18 or older, in-person pickup with a short librarian walkthrough and a signed agreement. Devices are factory-reset between borrowers.
  • PCs for People — low-cost refurbished computers, mail order only (there is no Phoenix storefront). You qualify at or below 200% of the Federal Poverty Guidelines or through a listed assistance program, and you will need a photo ID plus dated documentation — handwritten letters are not accepted. No proof of citizenship is required. (651) 354-2552.
  • Computers 2 Kids (Phoenix) — refurbished desktops from roughly $80 and laptops from roughly $100 for qualifying households.

Cheaper home internet, if you have an address

If you have a place to live and the problem is the bill, the main low-income offers are still running: Xfinity Internet Essentials is about $9.95 a month, and AT&T Access has a comparable program. Both allow the Lifeline discount to be applied on top, which can bring a monthly bill close to zero. Confirm current pricing and terms directly with the provider before signing up — these offers change.

Keeping a phone once you have one

Phone loss and theft are the normal case, not the exception, and they are the single most disruptive thing that can happen to a housing plan in progress. A few habits that survive it:

  • Write your account number and PIN on paper and keep the paper somewhere other than the phone. Without them, replacing or porting your number is far harder.
  • Keep your number. If you switch providers, ask to port your existing number rather than take a new one. Every caseworker, landlord, and clinic has the old one.
  • Write the important numbers down too. A contact list that only exists in a lost phone is gone.
  • Set up an email you can reach from any computer — a free web email works from a library machine even when your phone does not.
  • Charging: libraries, cooling centers, and day resource centers have outlets. In summer, our heat relief guide lists sites that are open, air-conditioned, and do not ask for ID.

One dead program to know about. The Connect Arizona Digital Navigator helpline — free one-on-one tech help — shut down on April 1, 2025 when its federal funding ended, though many directories still list its number. For hands-on help now, use a public library instead.

Call ahead before you travel. Library hours, loan programs, and provider offers change. Dollar figures on this page are current as of September 2026. If a number here is wrong, tell us and we will fix it. For anything urgent, dial 2-1-1 — free, 24/7, and they keep a current referral list.
