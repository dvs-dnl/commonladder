Homeless Veteran Services in Phoenix: HUD-VASH, the VA, and How to Get Help

By Common Ladder · May 29, 2026 · Updated September 14, 2026 · 6 min read

If you are a veteran experiencing or at risk of homelessness in Phoenix, the fastest call is the National Call Center for Homeless Veterans at 877-424-3838 (877-4AID-VET). It is staffed 24 hours a day, every day, and the VA counselor on the line will connect you to local Phoenix homeless veteran programs and stay with you until you have a next step.

If you are in crisis right now — or worried about a veteran who is — call the Veterans Crisis Line at 988 and press 1, or text 838255. You do not need to be enrolled in VA health care to use either line.

This guide walks through the front doors of Phoenix's veteran homelessness system: the Phoenix VA's homeless programs, HUD-VASH housing vouchers, SSVF rental help, transitional housing, and the local nonprofits that fill in around the VA.

Start at the Phoenix VA

Almost every VA-funded homeless program in Phoenix is entered through the Phoenix VA's Community Resource and Referral Center (CRRC). The CRRC — not the hospital switchboard — is the front door: it does the screening, the eligibility check, and the warm handoff to housing programs. You do not need to be enrolled in VA health care to be seen.

  • Phoenix VA Community Resource and Referral Center (CRRC)

1500 E. Thomas Rd, Suite 106, Phoenix, AZ 85014
Phone: 602-248-6040
Walk in Monday–Friday, 7:30 a.m.–4:00 p.m. Go early in the day — a same-day transitional housing referral is not guaranteed if you arrive late.

The Carl T. Hayden VA Medical Center (650 E. Indian School Rd, Phoenix, AZ 85012; main line 602-277-5551) is the medical center, not the homeless-programs intake. If you are already a patient there, ask your care team to connect you to the CRRC.

Bring your DD-214 (separation papers) if you have it. If you do not, CRRC staff can request a copy on your behalf — do not let a missing DD-214 stop you from walking in.

HUD-VASH (Housing Choice Voucher + VA case management)

HUD-VASH pairs a Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher with ongoing VA case management. For many veterans it is the most direct path from the street to a lease. Phoenix HUD-VASH vouchers are administered through the City of Phoenix Housing Department and the Housing Authority of Maricopa County, but you cannot apply to either directly for HUD-VASH — you must be referred through the VA.

  • Start with the CRRC at the Phoenix VA (above).
  • Eligibility is based on VA homeless verification, income, and willingness to engage with case management.
  • After referral, the housing authority will issue the voucher; you then have a set window to find a participating landlord.

If you already hold a HUD-VASH voucher and are stuck on the landlord-search step, ask your VA case manager about the landlord engagement specialists and the SOI (source-of-income) discrimination protections in the City of Phoenix.

SSVF (Supportive Services for Veteran Families)

SSVF is short-term rental assistance, deposit help, utility help, and case management for very-low-income veterans who are homeless or at imminent risk of losing their housing. SSVF can move quickly — sometimes within days — when documentation is in order. In Phoenix, SSVF is delivered by VA-funded grantees, including:

  • U.S.VETS — Phoenix — 3507 N. Central Ave., Suite 200, Phoenix, AZ 85012. SSVF direct line: 602-441-0612. Main: 602-717-6682. Outreach: 602-757-9723 or 602-859-7329. Operates outreach, SSVF rapid rehousing, transitional housing, and employment services.
  • Catholic Charities Community Services — Phoenix area; phone: 1-855-316-2229 (toll-free). Runs MANA House (below). Catholic Charities does not currently list an SSVF program for Maricopa County on its own site — for SSVF in Phoenix, call U.S.VETS at 602-441-0612.

If you are not sure which grantee covers your situation, the National Call Center at 877-424-3838 will route you to the right one.

Transitional Housing for Veterans

If you are not ready for a lease but need to come inside while you stabilize, the VA's Grant and Per Diem (GPD) program funds transitional housing. In Phoenix:

  • U.S.VETS — Phoenix — transitional and permanent housing at sites across Phoenix, with employment, mental health, and substance use services on site. This includes the M.D. Hawkins Veterans Center, 12027 N. 28th Dr., Phoenix, AZ 85029, opened in 2026, which has a dedicated wing for women veterans.
  • MANA House (Catholic Charities Community Services) — 2422 W. Holly St, Phoenix, AZ 85009. Phone: 602-254-6785. Peer-led transitional housing; veterans run much of the house themselves.

MANA House accepts referrals only from the Phoenix VA's Community Resource and Referral Center. Do not go to 2422 W. Holly St. without one — start at the CRRC (1500 E. Thomas Rd, Suite 106; 602-248-6040). U.S.VETS transitional housing also runs through VA referral.

County and State Resources

  • Arizona Department of Veterans' Services — 602-255-3373. Statewide claims help, state veteran benefits, and veteran cemetery services.
  • Maricopa County Human Services Department — 602-506-5911. The county department that handles veteran referrals, rent and utility help, and other assistance. (The county runs a referral portal rather than a standalone veteran services office.)

The Annual Phoenix Stand Down

Each year the Maricopa County StandDown brings dozens of providers under one roof at the Arizona State Fairgrounds. Veterans experiencing homelessness can get IDs, DD-214 copies, dental and vision care, legal help (including resolving outstanding warrants in a special StandDown court), VA enrollment, employment services, hygiene supplies, and direct referrals to housing programs.

The event is run by the Arizona Veterans StandDown Alliance, a program of the Arizona Housing Coalition. The 2026 Maricopa StandDown ran two days, March 12–13, 2026 — it is a late-winter/early-spring event, not a fall one. 2027 dates had not been announced as of September 2026; check azhousingcoalition.org/avsa for current-year dates and Valley shuttle pickup locations.

What to Expect

The veteran homeless system in Phoenix moves faster than the general adult system, but it still takes patience. The most important steps:

1. Call 877-424-3838 today — even just to be in the system.
2. Walk into the Phoenix VA CRRC (1500 E. Thomas Rd, Suite 106) within the next week, Monday–Friday 7:30 a.m.–4:00 p.m., with your DD-214 if you have it.
3. Stay reachable. Get a free Lifeline phone if you do not have one (Common Ladder's Resource Sequencing Navigator can walk you through it).
4. If your case manager goes quiet for more than a week, call the National Call Center again — they can re-engage on your behalf.

Service to this country earned you a place in this system. Use it.

Common Ladder is a civic homelessness resource platform for Phoenix, Arizona. We do not operate VA programs or determine eligibility. Always confirm program details with the Phoenix VA, the National Call Center for Homeless Veterans, or 211 Arizona before traveling to any address listed above.

Frequently asked questions

What is the fastest way for a homeless veteran to get help in Phoenix?

Call the National Call Center for Homeless Veterans at 877-424-3838 (877-4AID-VET). It is staffed 24 hours a day, every day, and the VA counselor will connect you to local Phoenix homeless veteran programs and stay with you until you have a next step.

Is there a crisis line specifically for veterans?

Yes. If you are in crisis right now — or worried about a veteran who is — call the Veterans Crisis Line at 988 and press 1, or text 838255. You do not need to be enrolled in VA health care to use either line.

How do I apply for a HUD-VASH housing voucher in Phoenix?

You must be referred through the VA — you cannot apply directly to the housing authority for HUD-VASH. Start at the Phoenix VA's Community Resource and Referral Center (CRRC), 1500 E. Thomas Rd, Suite 106, 602-248-6040, which does the screening and eligibility check; after referral, the housing authority issues the voucher.

What if I don't have my DD-214 (separation papers)?

Do not let a missing DD-214 stop you from walking in. Bring it if you have it, but if you do not, staff at the Phoenix VA's Community Resource and Referral Center (CRRC) can request a copy on your behalf.

What is the Phoenix Stand Down for homeless veterans?

The Maricopa County StandDown is an annual two-day event at the Arizona State Fairgrounds, run by the Arizona Veterans StandDown Alliance (a program of the Arizona Housing Coalition). It is held in late winter or early spring — the 2026 event ran March 12–13, 2026. Veterans experiencing homelessness can get IDs, DD-214 copies, dental and vision care, legal help, VA enrollment, employment services, and direct referrals to housing programs.
