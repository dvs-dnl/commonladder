Free and Low-Cost Dental Care in Phoenix: Where to Go Without Insurance

By Common Ladder · August 23, 2026 · 7 min read

A toothache is one of the fastest ways a hard month becomes a dangerous one. Dental pain makes it harder to eat, sleep, work, and interview, and an untreated infection can put you in the hospital. Yet most adult AHCCCS plans cover almost no routine dental work, and a private dentist visit starts at more money than many households see in a week. The good news: Phoenix has real options — a charity clinic with a $60 emergency extraction, sliding-fee community dentistry, a dental center built for people experiencing homelessness, and teaching clinics where a full cleaning costs $70. If you only want one number, dial 2-1-1 (free, confidential, 24/7) and say you need dental care.

If you are in pain right now

  • St. Vincent de Paul — Delta Dental of Arizona Oral Health Center — 420 W. Watkins Rd., Phoenix, AZ 85003 — (602) 261-6842 or (602) 261-6827. Non-patients can be seen for a one-time emergency visit on a space-available basis — typically an extraction to stop pain and infection, for a flat $60. Call first; emergency slots are limited and appointments cannot be rescheduled.
  • NATIVE HEALTH — walk-in dental emergency windows Monday–Friday, 8–10 a.m. and 1–3 p.m. at three clinics: Central (4041 N. Central Ave., Bldg. C, Phoenix), NHW Community Health Center (8800 N. 22nd Ave., Phoenix), and Mesa (777 W. Southern Ave.). One number for all sites: (602) 279-5262. Open to everyone, not only Native patients, with fees discounted by family size and income.
  • If you have AHCCCS: adults 21+ have an emergency-only dental benefit — exams, X-rays, extractions, and treatment of acute pain or infection, up to $1,000 per contract year (October 1–September 30). Call the member services number on your card and ask for a dentist taking emergency dental patients.

Ongoing care without insurance

St. Vincent de Paul’s adult dental lottery

SVdP’s charity clinic provides cleanings, fillings, crowns, root canals, and dentures to uninsured adults for a flat $25 per appointment (lab fees for dentures and crowns are discussed up front). Because demand far exceeds chairs, adults enter through a lottery: apply in person at 420 W. Watkins Rd. during open application months — January, March, May, July, September, and November — and you will get a call if selected for a screening. Children ages 3–18 without insurance can get comprehensive care and even orthodontics without the lottery; call (602) 261-6842.

NATIVE HEALTH’s sliding-fee dentistry

NATIVE HEALTH is a community health center that serves all patients regardless of ability to pay. Scheduled dental appointments run Monday–Friday, 8 a.m.–5 p.m. at all three clinics; ask a Patient Enrollment Specialist about the Discount Fee Program, which sets your price by family size and income. Services run from cleanings and fillings to root canals, extractions, and dentures, and the clinic can help arrange transportation to appointments. Call (602) 279-5262.

Brighter Way: dentistry on the Keys to Change Campus

Brighter Way Dental Center — 1300 W. Harrison St., Phoenix, AZ 85007, on the Keys to Change (Human Services) Campus — was founded to serve people experiencing homelessness, veterans, domestic violence survivors, and people with special needs, and now also offers reduced-fee general dentistry and implants to the wider community to fund that mission. Clinical hours are Monday–Thursday, 8:30 a.m.–5 p.m. (Friday is administrative only). Patients from the campus and partner agencies are usually connected by referral — if you are staying on or near the campus, ask your case manager, or call (602) 362-0744.

The cheapest cleanings in town: teaching clinics

  • Phoenix College Dental Clinic — 3144 N. 7th Ave., Phoenix, AZ 85013 — (602) 285-7323. The first 60-minute assessment is free; a full hygiene visit is a $70 base fee covering X-rays, an exam, an oral cancer screening, a gum assessment, and a cleaning. Students work under licensed faculty, so plan on four or more multi-hour visits — slow, thorough, and far below private prices. Payment is due before service.
  • Midwestern University Dental Institute — 5855 W. Utopia Rd., Glendale, AZ 85308 — (623) 537-6000, Monday–Friday, 8 a.m.–5 p.m. Student dentists supervised by faculty provide cleanings, fillings, crowns, dentures, extractions, root canals, and emergency care at significantly reduced fees.

A note on choosing: if you need pain stopped this week, use SVdP’s emergency clinic, NATIVE HEALTH’s walk-in windows, or your AHCCCS plan. If you need a mouth rebuilt — multiple fillings, dentures — the SVdP lottery and the teaching clinics are built exactly for that, but they take patience. Doing both in parallel is normal: get the emergency handled now, and put your name in for ongoing care the same month.

Verify before you go / call ahead. Phone numbers, hours, fees, and application windows change — the SVdP lottery months and NATIVE HEALTH’s emergency walk-in windows especially. Call before traveling to any address on this page. If a number is out of service, dial 211 for a current referral.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I get an emergency tooth pulled in Phoenix without insurance?

St. Vincent de Paul’s dental clinic at 420 W. Watkins Rd. sees non-patients for one-time emergency extractions on a space-available basis for $60 — call (602) 261-6842 or (602) 261-6827. NATIVE HEALTH takes walk-in dental emergencies Monday through Friday from 8–10 a.m. and 1–3 p.m. at its Phoenix and Mesa clinics, (602) 279-5262, with fees on a sliding scale. If you have AHCCCS, emergency dental care including extractions is covered up to $1,000 per contract year.

Does AHCCCS cover dental care for adults?

Only emergencies. For adults 21 and older, AHCCCS covers medically necessary emergency dental care — exams, X-rays, extractions, and treatment of acute pain or infection — up to $1,000 per member per contract year (October 1 through September 30). Routine cleanings, fillings, and dentures are generally not covered for adults. Members under 21 get comprehensive dental coverage. Call your AHCCCS health plan to find a dentist who takes it.

What is the cheapest way to get a dental cleaning in Phoenix?

Teaching clinics. Phoenix College’s Dental Clinic at 3144 N. 7th Ave. starts with a free 60-minute assessment, (602) 285-7323, and charges a $70 base fee that covers X-rays, an exam, an oral cancer screening, and a cleaning — expect four or more multi-hour visits, since students work under faculty supervision. Midwestern University’s Dental Institute in Glendale, (623) 537-6000, offers significantly reduced fees for a full range of care.

Is there a dentist for people experiencing homelessness in Phoenix?

Yes. Brighter Way Dental Center sits on the Keys to Change Campus at 1300 W. Harrison St. and was founded to serve people experiencing homelessness, veterans, and domestic violence survivors — call (602) 362-0744, Monday through Thursday 8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m. Patients are often connected through a referral from a campus or partner agency, so ask your case manager or call first. St. Vincent de Paul and NATIVE HEALTH also treat patients regardless of housing status or ability to pay.
