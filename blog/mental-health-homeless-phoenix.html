Mental Health and Substance Use Help in Phoenix When You're Unhoused: Where to Go Right Now

By Common Ladder · May 29, 2026 · Reviewed October 11, 2026 · 5 min read

If you're in crisis right now, you don't need insurance, an ID, or an address. Call or text 988 (Suicide and Crisis Lifeline) or call Arizona's 24/7 crisis line at 1-844-534-HOPE (1-844-534-4673). Both are free, both answer fast, and both can send a mobile team to where you are.

That's the most important thing on this page. Everything below is for the next steps — getting ongoing care, medication, detox, or a connection to a counselor — without needing a permanent address.

If it's a crisis right now

Call or text 988. It's the national Suicide and Crisis Lifeline. Free, confidential, available 24/7. You can call from any phone, including a stranger's. You do not need to be suicidal to call — overwhelmed, hearing things, scared, coming down, panicking all count.

Call the Arizona 24/7 crisis line (Solari Crisis Response Network): 1-844-534-HOPE (1-844-534-4673), or text HOPE to 44673. The 24/7 Crisis Response Network line 1-800-631-1314 also reaches a crisis team in Maricopa County. This is the local 24/7 line that can dispatch a Mobile Crisis Team to your location — a park, a sidewalk, a shelter, a gas station. The team is two trained behavioral health responders, not police. They will not arrest you. If you need more help than they can give on the spot, they can drive you to a stabilization center.

If you'd rather walk in: Connections Health Solutions runs Connections Phoenix (long known as the Urgent Psychiatric Care or UPC center) at 1201 S. 7th Ave., Unit 150, Phoenix, AZ 85007 — (602) 416-7600, 24/7, with no appointment needed. They take everyone, insured or not. Stays are short — usually under 24 hours — and they connect you to follow-up care before you leave.

If you need ongoing mental health care (not an emergency)

For something steadier — a counselor, medication, a treatment plan — the path runs through AHCCCS, Arizona's Medicaid program. People experiencing homelessness qualify in nearly every case, and AHCCCS staff have a simplified enrollment process that doesn't require an address. You will need somewhere to receive mail. The Key Campus (204 S. 12th Ave.) provides mail service — ask at the Brian Garcia Welcome Center — and CASS gives clients a mail address at 232 S. 12th Ave. General Delivery at a post office is another route, but which Phoenix station accepts it changes; call USPS at 1-800-275-8777 to confirm before relying on it.

In Maricopa County, AHCCCS behavioral health is administered by Mercy Care. For Mercy Care ACC-RBHA members, the line is 602-263-3000 or 1-800-624-3879, Mon–Fri 7 a.m.–6 p.m. If you have a Serious Mental Illness (SMI) designation, there is a separate 24/7 line: 602-586-1841 or 1-800-564-5465. Either can match you with a provider near you, including providers who specialize in working with unhoused clients.

Providers that take Mercy Care RBHA and have experience serving people experiencing homelessness:

  • Terros Health — multiple Phoenix locations, integrated primary and behavioral care. Main number: 602-685-6000.
  • Community Bridges Inc. (CBI) — therapy, psychiatric care, detox, and residential treatment. Access line: 1-877-931-9142.
  • Native American Connections — culturally specific behavioral health and housing, open to Native and non-Native clients. Main number: 602-254-3247.
  • Circle the City — healthcare specifically for people experiencing homelessness. Behavioral health therapy and integrated psychiatric care are at the Parsons Family Health Center, 3522 N. 3rd Ave. — 602-776-7676. The Downtown Family Health Center, 220 S. 12th Ave. — 602-258-8282 handles primary care. For mobile outreach in your neighborhood, call 602-516-6825.

You do not need to choose right. Call Mercy Care or any of these providers and they will route you.

If you're trying to stop using

You can detox safely without paying out of pocket. The two main paths in Phoenix:

Community Bridges Inc. (CBI) withdrawal management — 24/7 medically supervised withdrawal management (detox) for alcohol and opioids. Two sites CBI lists for this: Central City Addiction Recovery Center, 2770 E. Van Buren St., Phoenix, AZ 85008 (the Phoenix option) and the East Valley Addiction Recovery Center, 560 S. Bellview, Mesa, AZ 85204. Call the 24/7 Access to Care line, 1-877-931-9142, first — they will tell you which site has room and can arrange transport if you can't get there.

Native American Connections residential treatment — intake is 602-424-2060, and walk-in residential assessments are at 337 E. Virginia Ave., Bldg. B, Phoenix, Mon–Fri 8 a.m.–3 p.m. (call ahead). Which program you enter depends on who you are: Patina Wellness Center (337 E. Virginia Ave., Bldg. A) serves women, two-spirit people, pregnant and single parents, and families with children under 5; Patina Mountain Preserve (1431 E. Dunlap Ave.) serves men and two-spirit people. NAC does not advertise medical detox by name — ask at intake whether they can manage your withdrawal, and if not, call CBI above.

After detox, both providers can move you directly into residential treatment if you want it, without an insurance gap.

If you're using and not ready to stop, harm reduction supplies (clean syringes, naloxone/Narcan, fentanyl test strips) are free through Sonoran Prevention Works — outreach happens in Phoenix throughout the week. Call 480-442-7086, or for the Maricopa County mobile outreach route, (928) 292-1602. SPW asks you to text ahead before travelling far, because the schedule shifts.

If you witness an overdose, call 911. Arizona's Good Samaritan law (ARS 13-3423) protects you from drug possession charges if you call to save a life.

On the Key Campus (formerly the Human Services Campus)

If you're already at the Key Campus — 204 S. 12th Ave., Phoenix, run by Keys to Change, the organization formerly called the Human Services Campus — mental health is among the services its partner nonprofits provide on site. Ask at the Brian Garcia Welcome Center, 206 S. 12th Ave.; they'll connect you. Campus main line (602) 282-0853; to call the Welcome Center ahead, 602-883-4509. This is often the fastest path if you're already nearby.

What to expect when you call

You will not be put on hold for an hour. Crisis lines answer in under a minute. AHCCCS enrollment can usually be started by phone the same day, and a real provider visit can happen within a week of enrollment. If anyone tells you that you "need an address first," that is wrong — say you are experiencing homelessness and ask for the homeless enrollment process. Every agency on this page has one.

You have a right to care here. Use it.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I get help in a mental health crisis when I'm unhoused in Phoenix?

You don't need insurance, an ID, or an address. Call or text 988 (Suicide and Crisis Lifeline) or call Arizona's 24/7 crisis line at 1-844-534-HOPE (1-844-534-4673) — both are free, both answer fast, and both can send a mobile team to where you are.

Can I walk in for psychiatric care without an appointment or insurance?

Yes. Connections Health Solutions runs Connections Phoenix, long known as the Urgent Psychiatric Care (UPC) center, at 1201 S. 7th Ave., Unit 150, Phoenix, AZ 85007 — (602) 416-7600 — 24/7 with no appointment needed. They take everyone, insured or not; stays are usually under 24 hours, and they connect you to follow-up care before you leave.

How do I get ongoing mental health care without a permanent address?

Enroll in AHCCCS, Arizona's Medicaid program — people experiencing homelessness qualify in nearly every case, and there's a simplified enrollment process that doesn't require an address. In Maricopa County, AHCCCS behavioral health is administered by Mercy Care; the ACC-RBHA member line is 602-263-3000 or 1-800-624-3879, Mon-Fri 7 a.m.-6 p.m., and members with an SMI designation have a separate 24/7 line, 602-586-1841 or 1-800-564-5465.

Where can I detox in Phoenix without paying out of pocket?

You can get medically supervised withdrawal management without paying out of pocket. Community Bridges Inc. runs it 24/7 at the Central City Addiction Recovery Center, 2770 E. Van Buren St., Phoenix, and the East Valley Addiction Recovery Center, 560 S. Bellview, Mesa - call the 24/7 Access to Care line, 1-877-931-9142, first. Native American Connections takes residential intake at 602-424-2060, with walk-in assessments at 337 E. Virginia Ave., Bldg. B, Mon-Fri 8 a.m.-3 p.m.

Where can I get free naloxone or clean supplies if I'm not ready to stop using?

Harm reduction supplies - clean syringes, naloxone/Narcan, and fentanyl test strips - are free through Sonoran Prevention Works, with outreach throughout Phoenix during the week. Call 480-442-7086, or (928) 292-1602 for the Maricopa County mobile outreach route, to find that week's locations.
