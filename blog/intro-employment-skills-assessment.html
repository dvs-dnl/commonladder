You Are Not Missing a Skill. You Are Missing a Door.

By Common Ladder · September 1, 2026 · 4 min read

If you want the short version: open the Employment Skills Self-Assessment, tap the kinds of work you have done and whatever is making a job search hard right now, and it will tell you which free Phoenix program is built for that situation, the number to call, and what to say on the call. It takes about a minute, there is no score, and nothing you tap leaves your phone. Everything below is why we built it that way.

The readiness test that shouldn’t exist

The standard shape of employment help is a screening. Are you stable enough, sober enough, housed enough. Do you have the documents, the address, the clothes, the gap-free resume. Pass and you get the service; fail and you get a referral to become more ready and come back later.

The evidence has been pointing the other way for a long time. Individual Placement and Support — the supported-employment model with the strongest research base for people with serious mental illness and housing instability — is built on the opposite premise: zero exclusion. No readiness test, no preparatory track, start the actual job search fast and build the support around the person while they work. It roughly doubles competitive employment rates compared with the train-first approach, which we covered in more detail in our brief on what the supported employment research actually shows.

The other half of the picture: most people who need this help are already working, or recently were — more than half of sheltered homeless adults have formal earnings in a given year. The problem is rarely that someone never held a job. It is that the job ended, the housing went with it, and the path back runs through a maze of programs that all sound the same from outside.

What the tool actually does

It asks two questions, both optional, and it does not grade either one.

The first is what kinds of work you have done — construction, warehouse, kitchen, driving, caregiving, retail, cleaning, landscaping, repair, office, security, or none of those yet. Paid or unpaid, recent or years ago, three days or three years. Each answer produces a plain statement of what that experience opens up in the Phoenix labor market and what to ask a coach for by name. Tap “I’ve never held a formal job” and you get a real answer too: ask for career exploration and paid work experience, funded programs that exist precisely for that.

The second question is what is in the way — no ID, a record, a gap, a health condition, being a veteran, being under 25, no diploma, no phone or address, no ride, hard time with online applications, nothing to wear, or needing income as fast as possible. Naming any of those disqualifies you from nothing. It just changes which door the page sends you to, because in Maricopa County there genuinely are different doors, and walking through the wrong one costs a day and a bus fare.

The output is a starting point, an ordered list of moves, a “who to call” block with verified numbers and addresses, and a short script for the call — because the hardest part of asking for help is often the first fifteen seconds.

Where it sends people

The routing is deliberately narrow, and every listing was checked against the provider’s own website rather than a directory. St. Joseph the Worker (602-755-5627, walk in at 382 E. Palm Lane #150 in Phoenix or 1833 W. Main St. #128 in Mesa) is the default door for most situations: free job coaching, a written job plan, direct employer referrals, and the practical things almost nobody else covers — interview clothes, uniforms, steel-toed boots, hand tools, transportation help. They build plans around barriers including justice involvement and housing insecurity, by their own description.

ARIZONA@WORK is the public system: City of Phoenix job centers if you live in the city (602-495-6905 south, 602-495-6906 west), Maricopa County career centers if you live outside it (602-372-4200 west valley, 602-372-9700 east valley). Free advising, workshops, computers with a person sitting next to you, hiring events, and funding toward certifications. The comprehensive one-stops also run reentry services and vocational rehabilitation, which is why the tool tells you to ask for those by name rather than just showing up.

Beyond that: Vocational Rehabilitation (1-800-563-1221) when a health condition or disability is part of the picture, with a broader definition of disability than most people assume. The Goodwill Veteran Career Center (4005 N. 16th St., 602-535-4212, weekdays 9 to 4) for veterans, with a Disabled American Veterans service officer taking walk-ins Tuesdays and Thursdays. The Excel Center (602-535-4401) for adults without a diploma — a tuition-free public charter high school for people 18 and over, awarding an actual diploma rather than a GED, with free childcare and transportation help.

What it does not do

It does not promise same-day cash, because no honest static page can. It collects nothing: no name, no email, no sign-up, no analytics on your answers. Your taps live in your own browser so the page remembers them if you come back, and “Start over” erases them. It does not decide whether you are ready. And it will not be right about everything — hours shift, programs change, and the tool says so and tells you to call ahead.

If you would rather read than tap, the longer written version is Free Job Help in Phoenix When You’re Experiencing Homelessness. If you cannot reach anyone on this list, call 2-1-1 — free, 24 hours a day, statewide — and ask for employment services near you.

Verify before you go. Every phone number, address, and set of hours in the tool was checked against the provider’s own website on September 1, 2026. Programs change. Call ahead before you travel, especially if the trip costs you a fare you can’t spare.
